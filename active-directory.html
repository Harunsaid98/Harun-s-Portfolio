What I tested

Seven support cases

01

Domain health and DNS

Checked domain health and found that the domain controller was registering its Tailscale address in DNS. The client was actually using that address.

02

Separate admin credentials

Verified that the everyday account was not a local administrator. Administrative tools ran with separate credentials when elevation was required.

03

Sign-in auditing

Applied an audit policy through Group Policy and confirmed successful and failed logon events 4624 and 4625 on the client.

04

Delegated password resets

Allowed a helpdesk test account to reset Finance passwords, while confirming it could not reset HR passwords or perform domain-wide administration.

05

Missing Group Policy console

Installed the RSAT Group Policy Management tool after the management console was unavailable on the workstation.

06

Broken domain trust

Reproduced a trust relationship failure and repaired the workstation's secure channel without removing the PC from the domain.

07

Protected AD object

Investigated an administrator's Access denied error when deleting an account. The cause was accidental-deletion protection.

A closer look

Fixing a trust relationship failure

Problem

After a restart, the Windows workstation could no longer authenticate to the domain because its trust relationship had failed.

Investigation

I checked the secure channel rather than immediately removing and rejoining the computer account.

Resolution

I repaired the secure channel using Test-ComputerSecureChannel -Repair and retested domain access.

Takeaways

What I learned

Having a Group Policy configured is not the same as proving it applied. I used client-side checks, including gpresult and auditpol, to confirm results.

Permission tests also need to run as the affected user. One early test accidentally ran with Administrator rights, so I began checking whoami before testing delegated access.

Scope and limitations

This was a personal VMware lab with a single domain controller and evaluation software. It was not a production environment. The domain controller's extra Tailscale DNS registration was documented as an issue to clean up.
